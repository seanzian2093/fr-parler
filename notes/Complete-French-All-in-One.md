# Complete French All-in-One

## Chapter 1 - Articles

### The definite article with nouns

All nouns in French have a gender

- `musculine` or `feminine`
  - whether they refer to a person, an animal, a thing or an abstract notion

English has only one definite article `the`, French uses

- `le` for `masculine` nouns
  - e.g., {{le village, village}}, {{le pont, bridge}}
- `la` for `feminine` nouns
  - e.g., {{la ville, city}}, {{la région, region}}
- `les` for plural nouns, both `masculine` and `feminine`
  - e.g., {{les villages}}, {{les villes}}
- `le` and `la` are shortened to `l'` before singular noun or adjective that begins with a vowel sound or a muted **h**
  - `masculine`: {{l'océan, ocean}}, {{l'ami, friend}}
  - `fiminine`: {{l'île, island}}, {{l'autoroute, highway}}


### The indefinite and partitive articles with nouns

Indefinite articles(a, an or some in English) are

- `un` for `masculine` singular noun
  - e.g., {{un projet, a project/plan}}, {{un bâtiment, a building}}
- `une` for `feminine` singular noun
  - e.g, {{une maison, a house}}, {{une lampe, a lamp}}, {{une avalanche, an avalanche}}, {{une aubergine, an eggplant}}
- `une` or `un` for `masculine` and/or `feminie` nouns
  - e.g. {{un(e) architecte, an architect}}, {{un(e) artiste, an artist}}, {{un(e) journaliste, a journalist}}
- `des` for both `masculine` and `feminine` plural
  - e.g., {{des murs, walls}}, {{des balcons, balconies}}

Partitive article is used when exact quantity of an item is unknown

- always required in French
- in English, partitive article is often ommitted
  - `I want bread` instead of `I want some bread`

It is formed by combining `de` and the definite article

- `de` + `le` = `du`
- `de` + `l'` = `de l'`
- `de` + `la` = `de la`
- `de` + `les` = `des`
- `du`, `de la`, or `des` all become `de` in negative since quantity does not exist any longer
  - except when using with verb `etre`, in negative, partitive article is always used

### Accents

- Accent Aigu
  - Acute accent is used only with the vowel **e**. It indicates
  - the vowel should be pronounced as a _closed_ _e_, similar to _e_ in English word _bed_
    - e.g., {{le café, coffee}}, {{le céléri, celery}}
  - In some words, the initial **é** replaces an initial **s**. Latin **s** is still alive in English cognates.
    - {{l'éponge, sponge
}}

- Accent Grave
  - used on the vowels **a**, **e**, and **u**
  - more open pronunciation
    - e.g., **è** resembels the vowel sound in English word **bad**
    - e.g., {{à, at/in}}, {{après, after}}, {{où, where}}

- Accent Circumflex
  - used on **a**, **e**, **i**, **o**, and **u**
  - if the English cognate contains an **s** that is missing in the French word, this French word will need a circumflex accent.
    - e.g., {{le coût, cost}}, {{la forêt, forest}}, {{l'hôpital, hospital}}, {{la pâte}}

- Accent diaeresis, or **tréma**
  - used when two vowels are next to each other and the accented vowel represents a separate sound
    - i.e., both vowel must be articulated.
    - e.g., {{aïe, ouch}}, {{Noël, Christmas}}

- Cedilla, graphic sign, not an accent
  - used under the letter **c**, before vowle **a**, **o**, and **u**
    - not with vowels **e** or **i**
  - it changes the hard sound **k** (like **c** in **catalogue**) to soft sound {{ça}}(**th** in **this**, **that**)
    - e.g., {{la façade, facade/front}}

- h aspiré
  - Some nouns start with a silent **h**, of which
    - the article that precedes some needs an apostrophe, e.g., {{l'habitant, habitant}}
    - the article that precedes some does not need apostrophe, i.e., no liaison, {{la haie, hedge}}
      - this is called **h aspiré**

### Capitalization

Capitalization is more frequent in English than in French

- Nationalities: adjectives that refer to nationalities are not capitalized in French
  - e.g., {{Marie est française, Marie is French
}}

- Languages: languages are not capitalized in French
  - e.g. {{Ils apprennent l'arabe, They are learning Arabic
}}

- Locations: when writing an address, words for street, avenue, place and other names are not capitalized in French
  - e.g., 25, {{rue de l'Université, University Street}}

- Dates, time, days, months, and seasons: such words are not capitalized
  - e.g., {{Nous partirons le 5 décembre, we'll leave on December 5
}}

- Religions: names of religions are not capitalized except **l'Islam**
  - e.g., {{le christianisme, Christianity
}}

- Geographical nouns: Geographical names are capitalized in French.
  - e.g., {{l'Asie, Asia}}, {{l'Europe, Europe}}, {{les États-Unis, United States}}
  - if the geographical noun is composed of a generic name(_bay, cape, river, sea_, etc) and a specific name, only the specific name is capitalized
    - {{le cap Horn, Cape Horn
}}
    - {{la péninsule Ibérique, the Iberian Peninsula
}}
  - Some irregular:
    - {{l'Asie centrale, Central Asia}}, {{l'Asie Mincure, Minor Asia}}

- Addressing people: when writing to people in French, capitalize most titles
  - {{Monsieur Verneuil, Mr Verneuil}}, {{Madame Deneuve, Madam Deneuve}}, {{Monsiuer le Premier ministre, Mr Premier Minister}}

## Chapter 2 - Basic Gender Endings

### Basic Masculine Noun Endings

- The following endings **tend** to be masculine
  - **-age**, **-ige**, **-ège**, **-oge**, **-uge**
    - e.g., {{le fromage, cheese}}, {{le mariage, marriage/wedding}}
    - exceptions:
      - {{la cage, cage}}, {{la loge, loge/dressing room}}, {{la page, page}}
  - **-ail**, **-euil**
    - e.g., {{le fauteuil, armchair}}, {{le seuil, threshold}}
  - **-ain**
    - e.g., {{le bain, bath}}, {{le pain, bread}}
    - one exception: {{la main, hand}}
  - **-al**
    - e.g., {{le cheval, horse}}, {{le mal, evil}}
  - **-ament**, **-ement**
    - e.g., {{le branchement, connection}}, {{le départment, department}}
  - **-ard**
    - e.g., {{le canard, duck}}
    - some words ending in **-ard** may have a pejorative connotation
      - e.g., {{le bâtard, bastard}}, {{le chauffard, reckless driver}}, {{le clochard, tramp}}, {{le vantard, braggart}}
  - **-eau**, **-ou**
    - e.g., {{le bateau, boat}}, {{le bijou, jewelry}}
    - exceptions:
      - {{la peau, skin}}, {{l'eau, water}}

  - **-el**
    - e.g., {{l'appel, call}}, {{le caramel, caramel}}, {{le logiciel, software}}, {{le sel, salt}}

  - **-ent**, **-ant**
    - e.g., {{l'argent, money}}, {{le restaurant, restaurant}}
    - exception:
      - {{la dent, tooth}}
  
  - **-er**
    - e.g., {{le boulager, baker}}, {{le déjeuner, lunch}}

  - **-ier/yer**
    - e.g., {{le calendrier, calendar}}, {{le loyer, rent}}

  - **-at**, **-et**, **-t**
    - e.g., {{le débat, debate}}, {{le billet, ticket}}, {{le circuit, circuit/tour}}
    - import exceptions:
      - {{la dot, dowry}}, {{la forêt, forest}}, {{la mort, death}}, {{la nuit, night}}, {{la part, share}}, {{la plupart, most people/things}}
  
  - **-eur**
    - e.g., {{le coeur, hear}}, {{l'aspirateur, vacuum cleaner}}, {{le compositeur, composer}}, {{le réfrigérateur, refrigerator}}
  
  - **-ien**
    - e.g., {{le bien, good}}, {{le chien, dog}}, {{l'entretien, interview/upkeep/maintenance}}
  
  - **-illon**
    - e.g., {{le bouillon, broth/bubble}}, {{le papillon, butterfly}}

  - **-in**
    - e.g., {{le chagrin, grief}}, {{le vin, wine}}, {{le jardin, garden}}
    - exception: {{la fin, end}}
  
  - **-is**
    - e.g., {{le tennis, tennis}}, {{le fouillis, mess/jumble}}
    - exceptions: {{la souris, mouse}}, {{la vis, screw}}

  - **-isme**
    - e.g., {{le socialisme, socialism}}, {{le séisme, earthquake}}

  - **-oir**, **-oin**
    - e.g., {{le besoin, need}}, {{le miroir, mirror}}

  - **-on**, **-om**
    - e.g., {{le nom, name}}, {{le torchon, dish towel}}
    - exceptions: {{la boisson, beverage}}, {{la chanson, song}}, {{la cloison, partition}}, {{la cuisson, cooking}}, {{la façon, manner}}, {{la leçon, lesson}}, {{la livraison, delivery}}, {{la rançon, ransom}}, {{la trahison, betrayal}}

  - **-phone**
    - e.g., {{le téléphone, telephone}}
  
  - **-scope**
    - e.g., {{le trombinoscope, group photo}}
  
  - **-a**
    - e.g., {{l'agenda, agenda}}, {{le visa, visa}}
    - exceptions: {{la diva, diva}}, {{la saga, saga}}, {{la villa, villa}}, {{la polka, polka}}, {{la véranda, veranda}}

  - **-as**
    - e.g., {{le repas, meal}}, {{le matelas, mattress}}, {{le bras, arm}}

  - **-ème**, **-me**, **-ome**, **-ôme**, **-aume**, **-rme**, **-sme**
    - e.g., {{le dôme, dome}}, {{le problème, problem}}, {{le spasme, spasm}}, {{l'idiome, idiom}}, {{le royaume, kingdom}}, {{le terme, term}}, {{le poème, poem}}

  - **-ble**, **-cle**, **-gle**, **-ple**
    - e.g., {{l'angle, angle}}, {{le sable, sand}}, {{le câble, cable}}, {{le spectacle, show}}, {{le périple, journey}}, {{le temple, temple}}
    - exceptions: {{la boucle, buckle}}, {{la fable, fable}}, {{la règle, rule}}, {{la table, table}}
  
  - **-ac**, **-ak**, **-ic**, **-oc**, **-uc**
    - e.g., {{le tabac, tobacco}}, {{le kayak, kayak}}, {{le fric, money/cash}}, {{le bloc, block/writing pad}}, {{le truc, trick/thing/gimmick}}

  - **-g**
    - e.g., {{le sang, blood}}, {{le faubourg, suburb/neighborhood}}
  
  - **-o**, **-op**, **-ort**, **-os**, **-ot**, **-ours**, **-us**
    - e.g., {{le numéro, number}}, {{le sirop, syrup}}, {{le sport, sport}}, {{le dos, back}}, {{let mot, word}}, {{le cours, course}}, {{le jus, juice}}
